1.Executive Summary

September was the month the two governing variables carried over from August's report moved from "in principle" toward paper. On September 23 the sponsor and John Quinlan reached agreement on the partnership buyout in writing: Quinlan is paid $14.0 million (expenditures at a 10% IRR) plus $2.3 million (debt accrued to the sponsor), and the estimated $7.0 million of remaining profit splits evenly between the two, netting the sponsor roughly $3.5 million. Against that backdrop the sponsor moved to the second governing variable: selling the property. Closer review of the paperwork found the Stream/Clark East terms read less like a purchase than a long, buyer-favorable option to purchase, and a formal ten-point issues memo was sent October 1 identifying the specific terms that need to be countered before leaning further into that offer. Separately, retail lot pricing discounting moved forward on its own track in late September, independent of the whole-site sale conversation.

2.Key Development #1 — Quinlan Buyout: Terms Agreed, Still Unsigned by Quinlan

On September 23 the sponsor reported reaching final agreement with John Quinlan on the ITPH partnership. As relayed: (1) expenditures at a 10% IRR, totaling $14.0 million, paid to Quinlan; (2) debt accrued to the sponsor of $2.3 million, also paid to Quinlan; (3) an estimated $7.0 million of remaining profit, split equally between Quinlan and the sponsor, netting the sponsor roughly $3.5 million. The sponsor's same-day reply: "Sounds workable — get Quinlan to sign agreement, then get an offer accepted and a deposit to escrow." The email also flagged, separately, three competing purchase offers on the property at approximately $24 million.

On September 24 the underlying paper arrived: a 44-page restated Company Agreement of Bissonnet 136, LLC, carrying the agreed terms — Class A preferred return cut from 20% to 10% IRR, and the profit split flipped from 85/15 (Class A/sponsor) to 50/50. The sponsor signed (individually and as Metro Holdings' managing member) on September 23. John Quinlan / Dublin, Inc. has not signed any of his four blocks (Managing Member, and GP/Trustee for the Ireland FLP, International Interests, and Bolivar Equities entities), and the agreement's own §15.19 states it does not bind until all members execute — so despite the filename, this is not yet a completed agreement. Separately, the companion Assignment of the landfill permit interest (Metro Holdings → Bissonnet 136, LLC) is fully executed on the sponsor's side and does not require Quinlan's signature. Two drafting gaps were raised and accepted as-is by the sponsor: an Assignment Section 2 wording ambiguity (works in the sponsor's favor) and an Exhibit A membership table still showing the prior 99/1 split rather than the new 50/50 language — both left unresolved on the view that the parties' correspondence already establishes intent.

3.Key Development #2 — Three Competing Purchase Offers; Lead Offer Flagged as an Option, Not a Sale

On October 1 the sponsor circulated a completed comparison of all offers received to purchase the ITPH property, framing the decision around who can close quickest and commit the most capital upfront. All three were described as serious, capable counterparties, with the sponsor "inclined to pursue the deal with Stream Capital" for the co-venture opportunity it presents — a preference the sponsor restated at the October 2 advisory meeting, where Stream/Clark East was also noted to have asked about co-venturing directly.

Same day, review of the comparison matrix and the underlying LOI language produced a different read: the Stream Capital / Clark East offer, at $24.0 million the highest of the three, carries terms that function less like a sale and more like a buyer-controlled option to purchase — roughly 300 days of tie-up, a soft/conflicting earnest-money position, an unnamed building design that gates every permit and environmental question, and buyer exit rights at nearly every stage. A ten-point issues memo (ITPH Offer Review — Top 10) was sent October 1 identifying the specific terms to counter before committing further to that path. The memo's central ask: require the buyer to specify the project — site plan, building type, stories, foundations — before any permit contingency is negotiable, since refusal to specify is itself the answer on counterparty intent.

Why the Stream/Clark East terms are flagged: the LOI's own text conflicts on whether the deposit is refundable through the end of inspection or hard at the end of the permit period; the 120-day permit-confirmation period has no objective finish line ("obtainable" is buyer-controlled); no site plan or building type is specified despite the site's landfill-build-over exposure, which keeps environmental and remediation risk exposure open-ended; Stream Realty Capital's role as capital partner is referenced but not named in the LOI itself; and the whole-tract exclusivity functionally removes ITPH from the market for close to a year while the buyer carries little at risk. Clay Development, by contrast, is the cleanest and fastest of the three (no permit contingency, ~270-day close) but is the lowest-priced offer and its LOI lapsed September 27 without extension. Panattoni's $4.00/SF verbal indication has not yet been put in writing.

Recommended next moves (per the October 1 memo): require the buyer to state the project before any permit contingency is negotiable; replace "obtainable" permit language with named City of Houston / TCEQ approvals and a hard deemed-satisfied date; hard the deposit to a real, early milestone; hold the environmental position to disclosure-only; name the vesting entity and confirm Stream Realty Capital's committed role; and revive the Clay LOI while getting Panattoni's indication in writing, so the sponsor is never negotiating against a single, soft bid.

6.Retail Marketing & Lot Pricing

September's retail/lot-level activity ran as a track parallel to the whole-site sale conversation rather than in place of it. A September 27 exchange confirmed the sponsor is actively discounting current lot pricing to move product, with the sponsor characterizing the effort as "ITPH — cranking!" when asked directly whether any signed contracts existed yet at month-end (none did, per that exchange — interest without signature). The August-built buyer landing page (password-gated, eight diligence tabs) and the broker/cash-buyer outreach threads opened in August were not referenced again in September correspondence; no update on their status is available this cycle.

  • Two parallel sale conversations are now live - the whole-site institutional sale (three competing offers) and lot-level retail discounting are both active at the same time; keep them reconciled so a whole-site closing doesn't strand retail buyers mid-process, or vice versa.
  • The parcel carve-out in the Stream/Clark East LOI is not a safe hedge - per the October 1 issues memo, any parcel sale during the whole-tract negotiation changes the acreage, frontage, and value the buyer is pricing and triggers a three-way renegotiation; it should not be relied on as a way to keep retail options open during that negotiation.
