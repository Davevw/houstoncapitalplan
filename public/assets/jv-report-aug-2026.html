1.Executive Summary

August was defined by two developments that together reset the ITPH capitalization picture. First, the JV partner buyout with John Quinlan — the gating item for financing, sale, and permit control — reached a documented impasse in the first week of the month and was then taken to a face-to-face meeting in Puerto Rico (August 19-21) built specifically to resolve it. Post-trip correspondence indicates the negotiation reached agreement in principle; definitive terms were not yet documented at month-end. Second, the infrastructure budget was re-estimated by the general contractor from $23 million to $33.0 million all-in, with the MUD reimbursement projection rising to $28.3 million and total projected proceeds to $67.7 million. Both items now govern every lender, buyer, and partner conversation on the project.

Around those two items, August was an outreach and readiness month: the Kennedy Funding letter of interest remained on hold pending the buyout, a cash-buyer investor group and a named retail-center buyer were introduced, an AI-assisted broker outreach campaign produced one qualified whole-site lead, a password-gated buyer landing page went live, and an internal accounting review flagged that ITPH has no financial ledger on the sponsor's own books — a diligence gap to close before the asset is presented as investable.

2.Key Development #1 — Quinlan Buyout: Impasse to Agreement in Principle

The month opened with the partner negotiation in writing for the first time. John Quinlan's position, set out in late July and carried into August, had two stated non-negotiables: a minimum 10% per annum compounded return on his capital (reduced from the original 20% IRR / 85%-of-profits structure, but firmly above the sponsor's 8% simple-interest offer), and return of the landfill/development permits to him rather than their treatment as the sponsor's equity contribution into a new entity. He also expects $25 million-plus of infrastructure to be reimbursed through the MUD, and noted long-standing personal loans to the sponsor, one secured against personal property.

The sponsor's response accepted the 10% compounded return in principle and proposed rolling Quinlan's capital and debt into the approximately $15 million payout number, but held two lines: the permit is the sponsor's equity contribution and cannot be assigned out before the new entity is capitalized, and the personal security must be released with any future security coming from the new JV. On August 3 the sponsor described the negotiation as an impasse and raised the possibility that the MUD directors' meeting needed to move the November vote forward could be affected if the partner issue was not resolved — tying the buyout directly to public-finance timing.

The sponsor's August 19-21 Puerto Rico trip was planned around a direct sit-down with Quinlan, supported by a written meeting plan covering current buyout and financing status, objectives in priority order, and an approach to reach agreement in principle in the room. Post-trip correspondence on August 24 characterized Houston as settled and congratulated the sponsor on getting the hard one done. As of August 31, no term sheet or executed buyout document had been circulated; the working assumption for this report is agreement in principle with documentation to follow in September.

3.Key Development #2 — Infrastructure Budget Re-Estimated to $33.0M

On August 7 the sponsor advised that the infrastructure budget could rise by roughly $6 million against the $23 million carried in the model, driven by TCEQ requirements for exporting fill material from retention areas, and that three buyout investor sources were evaluating the project. On August 9-10 Metro Structures delivered its updated opinion of probable cost including a 5% contingency, and the capital model was updated to a $33.0 million all-in development cost. The MUD reimbursement projection was prorated upward to $28.3 million and total projected proceeds to $67.7 million. The sponsor's framing is that return metrics should not change materially because reimbursable infrastructure scales with the MUD; that framing depends on the November 2026 MUD vote and on the reimbursement scaling as assumed.

6.Retail Marketing & Buyer Outreach

Outreach shifted from lender-only to a dual buyer / capital track in August. An AI voice-agent campaign was relaunched August 6 against the de-duplicated LoopNet contact list (51 contacts, 43 dialed), with leads routed directly to the sponsor team and the script tightened to whole-project or large-parcel acquisition interest only. Result: one qualified broker lead with whole-site interest. On August 19 a password-gated buyer landing page went live, exposing eight diligence tabs (Dashboard, Lot Schedule, Cash Flows, Capital Stack, Financial Model, Site Plan, MUD Analysis, Design Concepts) while keeping expenditures, deemed-capital treatment, admin tools, and the full capital model spreadsheet internal. The first named buyer contact received access the same day.

  • Retail remains intersection-led - no change to the June/July reconfiguration premise; highest-value pads stay at visible corner and intersection frontage.
  • Whole-site interest is emerging - both the broker lead and the cash-buyer group are oriented to the full 99 net acres rather than individual parcels, which supports presenting ITPH as a single recapitalization or acquisition opportunity alongside the parcel program.
  • Materials must reflect the $33.0M budget - the landing page financial model and MUD analysis were updated; earlier tear sheets in circulation should be superseded.

September's job is documentation and alignment: paper the Quinlan terms, put every counterparty on the same updated numbers, re-open the lender tracks, and close the financial-controls gap so the asset can withstand diligence as an investable position.
