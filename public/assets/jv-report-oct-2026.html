1.Executive Summary

October resolved the question September left open: which of the three competing purchase offers is real. Panattoni formally withdrew on October 30 — a written pass from Riley Hix citing their institutional capital partners' preference for environmentally clean sites, after their Houston team's environmental and risk management groups reviewed the deal. Panattoni's $4.00/SF indication never made it to a written LOI. Stream Capital / Clark East remains flagged "unlikely" — the sponsor's own October 28 assessment is that Stream, like Panattoni, depends on outside capital neither has been forthright about. That leaves Clay Development as the clear leader, and their position improved materially over the month: a revised LOI received October 29 raised the price to $22,008,037 ($3.70/SF, up from $21,413,225) and increased earnest money to $190,000 total (up from $50,000). Clay's broker (NAI Robert Lynn) has stated this is their best and final offer.

Separately, two other developments moved the project forward this month. On October 19, an operation to import 160,000 cubic yards of certified-clean fill onto the site began — placed in low areas and former golf-course ponds — before the sponsor had a hold-harmless agreement in hand or Metro Structures (the site's general contractor) confirmed as present to oversee placement; both are now being arranged after the fact. And on October 27, the project team presented to the Houston-area neighborhood association on the landfill-to-development plan, with environmental, geotechnical, and engineering representatives (Kimley-Horn) on hand to field public questions — a visible diligence moment worth carrying into investor materials. The Quinlan buyout, agreed in principle in September, saw no documented movement in October; it remains unsigned by John Quinlan / Dublin, Inc. as of month-end.

2.Key Development #1 — Field Narrows to One: Clay Leads, Panattoni Out, Stream Unlikely

The month's central story is the collapse of three live offers down to one credible path. On October 28, the sponsor reported that, after further discussions, "it's becoming clear" Clay Development is the likely contender — both Stream and Panattoni rely on outside capital sources that neither has been willing to identify. The sponsor met with Clay's principals in Houston that same day. A revised LOI Comparison Matrix was circulated October 28 reordering Clay into the lead position, with Stream and Panattoni both flagged unlikely and the reasoning noted for each.

On October 29, Clay's counter-offer landed (via NAI Robert Lynn, in response to the sponsor's October 16 track-changed comments on their prior LOI) — "best and final," per their broker Josh Carl. The sponsor met with Clay again that week and asked them to tighten the LOI further, with a further-improved or final version expected by October 30. On October 30, Panattoni's Riley Hix sent a written pass: after review by their Environmental and Risk Management teams, the site "presents challenges... from both a capitalization and insurance standpoint," noting their institutional capital partners prefer environmentally clean sites and that they've had similar issues with sites carrying any oil-and-gas history. The sponsor's reply characterized the site as "environmentally certified," not merely clean, pushing back lightly on the characterization while accepting the pass.

3.Key Development #2 — 160,000 CY Clean-Fill Import Begins Ahead of Paper

On October 19, the sponsor learned — from a local contact, not through the project's own GC channel — that an operation to import 160,000 cubic yards of certified-clean fill onto the ITPH site had commenced, with material being placed in low-fill areas and former golf-course ponds. Metro Structures (site GC) had not been informed the start date was Monday and had not yet seen the hold-harmless agreement governing the placement; their primary concerns are hold-harmless language, and liability for road damage or material tracking from trucks. The sponsor favors a hold-harmless agreement but had not arranged one in advance of the start date, and is now working to get Metro's Steve Meyer and SKA's Michael Schultz looped in to oversee where the fill is placed and confirm it matches the intended plan.

Why this matters: 160,000 cubic yards of certified-clean fill delivered at no cost to the project is a meaningful in-kind contribution toward the site's development readiness — but starting the operation before the hold-harmless agreement and GC oversight were in place is a documentation and liability gap that should be closed immediately, not retroactively, especially with a live sale process underway.

4.Community Engagement & Public Relations

On October 27, the project team presented to the Houston-area neighborhood association on converting the 136-acre former landfill into commercial, residential, retail, and light-flex space. Environmental, geotechnical, and engineering representatives (Kimley-Horn) were present to answer public questions directly about the site's future and safety. The sponsor framed it as a model of coordinated teamwork and addressed community concerns head-on. Photos from the meeting have been filed into the project data vault under "Community Meeting."

Investor-relevant takeaway: visible, in-person diligence in front of the public — with named technical experts fielding questions — is exactly the kind of record that supports the landfill-to-development thesis to lenders, buyers, and the MUD electorate ahead of November's vote. This has been noted for inclusion in investor-facing materials going forward.
5.Quinlan Buyout — No Movement

No correspondence addressing the Quinlan buyout or the restated Bissonnet 136 Company Agreement was identified in October. As of month-end, the position is unchanged from September: terms were agreed September 23 ($14.0 million expenditures at 10% IRR, plus $2.3 million accrued debt, plus a 50/50 split of an estimated $7.0 million remaining profit, netting the sponsor roughly $3.5 million), the sponsor has signed the restated Company Agreement, and John Quinlan / Dublin, Inc. has not executed any of his four signature blocks. Per the agreement's own §15.19, it does not bind until all members sign. With the purchase-offer field now narrowed to Clay, getting Quinlan's countersignature in hand becomes more urgent, not less — a signed sale needs a settled ownership structure behind it.
